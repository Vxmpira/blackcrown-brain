<h2>What a killzone is</h2>
<p>The term comes from the ICT (Inner Circle Trader) method. It names time windows, usually tied to major market sessions, where the method says liquidity and directional moves are more likely to appear. A killzone is a time filter. It does not tell you direction, and it does not promise a move.</p>
<p>Most traders who use the term treat it as a place to focus attention, not a rule that forces entries. The window is a reason to look, nothing more.</p>

<h2>Why volume tends to cluster there</h2>
<p>Volume follows participants. Index futures are traded by funds, banks, market makers, and retail traders who open their screens when their own market is open. When London opens, European desks join. When the New York cash session opens at 9:30 AM Eastern, US participants arrive in force, and scheduled data such as employment and inflation releases tends to land in the morning.</p>
<p>That overlap is the core idea behind the windows. More participants mean more orders, and more orders mean more chances for price to move through prior levels. The windows are a map of when the crowd is awake, not a map of where price will go.</p>
<p class="note">Clustering is a tendency, not a guarantee. Quiet sessions happen, and busy sessions can fail to follow through. Nothing here predicts a specific outcome.</p>
