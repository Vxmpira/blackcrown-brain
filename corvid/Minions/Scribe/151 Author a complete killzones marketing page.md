  <h2>What a killzone is</h2>
  <p>Killzones are time windows that ICT-style methods treat as higher activity periods. The idea is simple: liquidity tends to move when major financial centers open, overlap, or close. Traders use these windows to focus their attention, not to guarantee anything. A killzone is a place to look, not a signal to act.</p>
  <p>Index futures such as ES and NQ trade nearly around the clock, so the question is never whether the market is open. The question is which part of the day you are watching, and what else was happening when that window started.</p>

  <h2>Timing alone is not enough</h2>
  <p>A killzone tells you when. It does not tell you what happened before the window opened, or whether a major release is about to hit. A London open on a quiet day reads very differently from a London open that lands an hour before a rate decision. Most of the mistakes we see come from reading the clock and ignoring the context.</p>
