<h1>Premium and Discount: Where Price Sits Inside a Range</h1>

<p>Every move has a start and an end. Between those two points sits a range, and price spends its time moving up and down inside it. Premium and discount are two ways of describing where price sits within that range. They are simple ideas. Most of the work is in knowing which range you are looking at.</p>

<h2>Start with the range</h2>
<p>A range is the high and the low of a chosen swing. Traders in the ICT tradition often draw it from a significant swing low to a significant swing high, or the reverse. Once you have the two points, the range is the space between them.</p>
<p>The range is only a container. It does not tell you direction, and it does not tell you what happens next. It tells you where the boundaries are, and that is all.</p>

<h2>Equilibrium is the midpoint</h2>
<p>Equilibrium is the exact middle of the range. Take the high, take the low, add them together, and divide by two. That number is equilibrium, sometimes written as the 50 percent level.</p>
<p>Equilibrium is the dividing line. Everything above it sits in premium. Everything below it sits in discount. Price can cross it many times in a session. Crossing it does not mean anything by itself.</p>

<h2>Discount: below the midpoint</h2>
<p>Discount is the lower half of the range. In ICT teaching, the idea is that price below equilibrium is relatively cheap compared with the range it is in. Some traders look for buying opportunities in discount, and they combine that with other conditions before doing anything.</p>
<p>Being in discount does not make a level cheap in any absolute sense. Price can fall through the discount zone and keep falling. The range can also be replaced by a bigger one. Discount describes location. It does not describe value.</p>

<h2>Premium: above the midpoint</h2>
<p>Premium is the upper half of the range. The same logic runs in reverse. Price above equilibrium is relatively expensive compared with the range it sits in. Some traders look for selling opportunities in premium, again only with other conditions in place.</p>
<p>Premium is not a short signal. Price can rise through it and keep rising. Treat it as a description of where price is, and nothing more.</p>

<h2>Why context matters</h2>
<p>The zones only make sense once you know which range you are using. A range drawn from a small intraday swing will give you one set of zones. A range drawn from a daily swing will give you another. Both can be correct. They answer different questions.</p>
<p>Before you use premium or discount, ask three things:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which swing defines this range, and is it significant on the timeframe you trade?</li>
<li>Where is the higher timeframe trend, and does this zone agree with it?</li>
<li>What other levels sit nearby, such as session highs, lows, or prior day levels?</li>
</ul>
<p>A discount zone inside a strong downtrend is a different thing from a discount zone inside a balanced session. The label is the same. The situation is not.</p>
