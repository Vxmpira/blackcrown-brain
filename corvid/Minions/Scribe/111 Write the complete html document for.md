<h2>What a killzone is</h2>
<p>A killzone is a fixed window of hours tied to a major financial center. ICT concepts describe the London and New York sessions as the windows where liquidity often enters the market in larger amounts. The word "killzone" is a label from that study tradition, not a measured guarantee. Nothing about a window forces price to do anything. It only marks where a student of the method chooses to focus attention.</p>
<p>The common idea is that the opening hour of a major session can bring volume, range, and direction shifts. Whether that happens on a given day depends on news, positioning, and the market itself.</p>

<h2>Why beginners care</h2>
<p>Most new traders watch price without a clock. They see a move and cannot say whether it started in a quiet hour or during a busy one. Killzones give structure to that question. They ask you to note the time, mark the prior session range, and watch what price does when a major window opens.</p>
<p>The practical benefit for a beginner is a habit of context. Before you judge a level, you check which session produced it and whether the market is in an active window. That habit is useful whatever method you end up using.</p>
<p>The limit matters too. A killzone is a lens for study. It does not tell you what to buy or sell, and it does not replace your own risk rules.</p>
