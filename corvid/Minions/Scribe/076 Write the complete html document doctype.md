    <h1>What moves the market?</h1>
    <p class="lede">Index futures move on a short list of forces. Some are scheduled. Some are not. Most of the noise is just the same few forces arriving at different speeds. This page names them plainly, so you can watch for them on your own terms.</p>

    <h2>The short answer</h2>
    <p>The market moves when new information arrives that changes what buyers and sellers expect. Price is the place where that change becomes visible. The hard part is not knowing that information matters. The hard part is knowing which information, and when.</p>
    <p>No single factor explains every move. Anyone who says otherwise is selling a story. This page is not a signal, a forecast, or a promise.</p>

    <h2>Six forces, in plain terms</h2>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>1. Scheduled economic data</h3>
      <p>Inflation prints, jobs reports, retail sales, and manufacturing surveys come out on a calendar. The calendar is public. The surprise is not. Markets price in an expected number, then react to the gap between expected and actual. The reaction often lasts minutes. Sometimes it lasts days.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>2. Central bank tone</h3>
      <p>Rate decisions matter, but wording often matters more. Speeches, minutes, and press conference answers shift expectations about future rates. Index futures tend to respond to changes in those expectations, not to the rate alone.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>3. Mega cap earnings</h3>
      <p>A handful of very large companies carry a big share of major index weight. When one of them reports, the index can move even if most of the market is quiet. Watch the weight, not just the headline.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>4. Headlines</h3>
      <p>Geopolitical events, policy announcements, and corporate news reach the tape fast. Many headlines fade. Some change the outlook for a sector or for the whole market. The skill is sorting one from the other, quickly, without panic.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>5. Liquidity and sessions</h3>
      <p>Index futures trade nearly around the clock, but not with equal depth at every hour. Overnight sessions, the cash open, and the cash close each carry different participants and different volume. The same headline can move price very differently at 2 a.m. and at 10 a.m.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="driver">
      <h3>6. Positioning</h3>
      <p>When many participants hold the same side of a trade, small surprises can cause outsized moves as positions unwind. Positioning is hard to see directly. It shows up indirectly, in how price reacts to news that should have been ignored.</p>
    </div>
