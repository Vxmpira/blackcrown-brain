<h1>SMT Divergence in Index Futures: A Plain Guide</h1>
<p class="lede">SMT stands for Smart Money Technique. It is a simple comparison, not a secret. You look at two or three related index futures at the same time and check whether they agree about the highs and lows. When they stop agreeing, that is the divergence.</p>

<h2>The basic idea</h2>
<p>E-mini S&amp;P 500 (ES), E-mini Nasdaq 100 (NQ) and E-mini Dow (YM) usually move in the same general direction. They are built from large, overlapping groups of stocks, so they tend to agree most of the time.</p>
<p>Agreement is the normal state. A divergence is what happens when one market does something the others do not confirm.</p>

<h2>What a divergence looks like</h2>
<p>Here is the usual pattern. Price is pushing up or down toward a level. One index prints a new high or a new low. The other index does not. It stops short of its own prior extreme.</p>
<p>That gap is the divergence. The indexes are no longer telling the same story at that point.</p>

<h2>What the cracked correlation can signal</h2>
<p>When one index breaks a level and the other does not follow, the market may be showing you that buying or selling pressure is not spread evenly. Some traders read this as a sign that the move may be weak. Others read it as a sign that a reversal could be forming. Neither reading is guaranteed.</p>
<p>A divergence is information. It is not an instruction.</p>

<h2>Reversal context</h2>
<p>In a reversal read, the divergence appears after an extended move. One index sweeps a high, the others stay below theirs, and the market shows signs of stalling. A trader using this as one input might watch for weakness before any change in direction. Plenty of divergences fail. Price keeps going.</p>

<h2>Continuation context</h2>
<p>In a continuation read, the divergence appears inside a trend. A pullback happens, one index dips to a new low, and the others hold. The trend may be healthy and the weak index may simply be lagging. Here the divergence is a warning that the move is not unanimous, not a call to fade the trend.</p>

<h2>Common beginner mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Treating every small disagreement as a signal. Small gaps are normal noise.</li>
<li>Ignoring the timeframe. A divergence on a one minute chart means less than one on a higher timeframe.</li>
<li>Forgetting the session. Overnight and regular hours can disagree for reasons that have nothing to do with the trade you think you see.</li>
<li>Assuming the divergence has to resolve in a specific direction.</li>
<li>Skipping risk rules because the setup looked clean in hindsight.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to read it honestly</h2>
<p>Mark the highs and lows on all the indexes you compare. Check whether they confirmed each other. Write down what happened next, including the times it failed. Over time you learn how often a divergence in your market actually changed anything. That record is the only real test.</p>
