  <h1>ICT Killzones for Index Futures</h1>
  <p>An ICT killzone is a time window when price often moves with more energy than the hours around it. Traders who study Inner Circle Trader concepts use killzones to decide when to look at a chart closely. The idea is simple. The concept is not a promise of any outcome.</p>
  <p>This page explains the two killzone windows most commonly taught, shows them on a timeline, and explains how the Korvus session context and the LodeStone sessions layer display them. Nothing here tells you what to trade.</p>

  <div class="chips">
    <span class="chip">London killzone</span>
    <span class="chip">New York killzone</span>
    <span class="chip">Eastern Time</span>
    <span class="chip">Index futures</span>
  </div>

  <h2>The two windows, in Eastern Time</h2>
  <p>Killzone times are usually written in New York time because the New York clock is the reference most index futures traders watch. These are the standard teaching windows:</p>
  <table>
    <tr><th>Session</th><th>Killzone window (ET)</th><th>What it marks</th></tr>
    <tr><td>London</td><td>2:00 AM to 5:00 AM</td><td>The London open and early European liquidity</td></tr>
    <tr><td>New York</td><td>7:00 AM to 10:00 AM</td><td>The New York open and the first major US data window</td></tr>
  </table>
  <p class="muted">Windows are teaching conventions. Your broker, your data feed, and your own notes may use different edges. Check what your platform shows before you rely on any time.</p>
