<h1>SMT Divergence for Index Futures: A Plain Guide</h1>
<p class="lede">SMT divergence is one of the simplest comparisons an index futures trader can make. It asks a single question: did the market confirm the move, or did one index break out while its partner failed to follow?</p>

<h2>What SMT divergence means</h2>
<p>SMT stands for Smart Money Technique. The idea is old and simple. Index futures that move together, such as the S&amp;P 500 (ES), the Nasdaq 100 (NQ), the Dow (YM) and the Russell 2000 (RTY), usually trend in the same direction. When they stop agreeing at a turning point, that disagreement is the divergence.</p>
<p>The most common read works like this. Price pushes to a new high or a new low on one index. A correlated index is asked to do the same thing at the same moment. If it does not, the two charts disagree. That gap is the SMT divergence.</p>

<h3>Bearish example in plain terms</h3>
<p>ES prints a higher high above an earlier swing. NQ does not. Its high stays below the prior swing high. The stronger index made a new high and the weaker one refused to confirm it. Traders who study this pattern call it a possible sign of weakening buying pressure. It is a observation, not a signal to act on by itself.</p>

<h3>Bullish example in plain terms</h3>
<p>RTY sweeps a prior low and dips below it. ES holds above its matching low. The smaller index probed for liquidity while the larger one stayed firm. Again, this is a description of disagreement between instruments, nothing more.</p>

<h2>Why it matters to index traders</h2>
<p>A single chart can show a breakout that looks convincing. Comparing it with a correlated index adds a second opinion from the same market. Many traders use SMT divergence as one piece of context, alongside session levels, structure and their own risk plan. It is not a complete system, and it does not work every time. Divergences often appear without a reversal, and reversals often happen without a clear divergence.</p>

<h2>A beginner's checklist for reading SMT</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Pick two or three correlated index futures and open them on the same timeframe and timezone.</li>
<li>Mark the most recent swing high or low on each chart.</li>
<li>Watch for one index to take that level while the others do not confirm.</li>
<li>Note the time, the session, and what the other instruments are doing at that moment.</li>
<li>Write down what you saw before you decide anything. Review it later against outcomes you actually recorded.</li>
</ol>
