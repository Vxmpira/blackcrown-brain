<h1>Cross Index SMT Divergence: What ES, NQ and YM Are Telling You</h1>
<p class="lede">Three correlated index futures rarely disagree for long. When one of them makes a new extreme and the others do not confirm it, that gap has a name: SMT divergence. Here is how the pattern is built, what it does and does not show, and how Korvus keeps watch on it.</p>
<div class="chips">
<span class="chip">ES</span><span class="chip">NQ</span><span class="chip">YM</span><span class="chip">Smart Money Technique</span><span class="chip">Session highs and lows</span>
</div>

<h2>What SMT divergence means</h2>
<p>SMT stands for Smart Money Technique, a term from ICT style price analysis. The idea is simple. The S&amp;P 500 (ES), the Nasdaq 100 (NQ) and the Dow 30 (YM) are built from overlapping large cap stocks. They move together most of the time. Each one has its own shape, but the swings line up.</p>
<p>A divergence happens when that alignment breaks at a key level. One contract sweeps a prior high or low, while another fails to make the matching move. The market is telling you the contracts disagree about where liquidity sits.</p>
<p>That disagreement is the observation. It is not a prediction, and it does not say what price will do next.</p>

<h2>The three contracts</h2>

<p>Pairs matter. ES against NQ is the most watched comparison. Adding YM gives a third reference, so a single contract failing to confirm can be checked against two others.</p>

<h2>How the pattern is usually read</h2>
<p>Most traders who study SMT start with the same checklist. The point is to understand the structure, not to memorize a trigger.</p>
<div class="panel">
<p><strong>1. Mark the level.</strong> A prior session high, low, or swing point that the contracts can be compared against.</p>
<p><strong>2. Watch for the sweep.</strong> One contract trades through the level.</p>
<p><strong>3. Check the others.</strong> Do the remaining contracts also trade through, or do they stall short of it?</p>
<p><strong>4. Note the mismatch.</strong> A stall, a shallow push, or a close back inside the range is the divergence.</p>
<p><strong>5. Record what happened next.</strong> Logging outcomes over many sessions is how you learn what the pattern does in your own market.</p>
</div>
<p class="note">Steps are for education. Whether a divergence matters depends on context, timeframe, liquidity and your own risk rules. Backtest before you trust any pattern.</p>
