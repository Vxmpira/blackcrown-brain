<h1>SMT Divergence Explained for Index Futures Traders</h1>
<p>SMT divergence, smart money technique divergence, is a crack between correlated indices. ES prints a new high while NQ refuses to follow, or YM sweeps a low that the others never touch. When instruments that normally move together stop agreeing, one of them is usually lying, and the disagreement itself is information.</p>

<h2>Why the crack matters</h2>
<p>Index futures share the same macro drivers. When they split at a swing point, it often marks engineered liquidity: price pushed into stops on one index without real participation across the complex. Traders use the divergence as confluence, not a signal. It tells you where conviction is thin, not where price must go.</p>

<h2>Reading it inside a session</h2>
<p>Divergence at 3 a.m. in a dead tape means little. The same split during the London or New York open, at a prior day high, next to resting liquidity, is a different animal. Context is most of the edge, which is why checking it by hand across three charts at once is where most traders lose the thread.</p>
