# Short-form pacing for 15 second Monolith clips

**Research note:** I did not run a live survey. These points come from established short-form practice and general video generation behavior. Verify current platform trends before treating any of them as settled.

## Findings

**Hook and motion**
- Start moving in frame 1. No fade in, no static opening frame. Motion in the first second is the scroll stopper.
- The opening shot should carry the strongest scale contrast: a tiny silhouette against the megastructure.

**Shot structure**
- Two to four shots across 15 seconds is the sweet spot. More cuts than that give generated footage more chances to drift or morph.
- Hold each shot at least 2 seconds so the center word overlay has time to register before the next cut.
- Keep one idea per shot. Monolith works because each frame reads as a single statement.

**Camera**
- Slow push ins, slow dollies, and gentle vertical tilts hold up well in generated video. Whip pans, fast orbits, and handheld shake tend to smear.
- Let the megastructure dominate the frame. Camera motion should feel heavy, not frantic.

**Light and color**
- Cold monochrome with hard directional light and atmospheric haze gives depth and reads cleanly on small phone screens.
- Reserve the pink for the final shot only. A single accent used once works as the payoff and the brand signature.

**Pacing rhythm**
- Pacing comes from the cut points and the hold times, not from motion speed. Slow footage with deliberate cuts feels more premium than fast footage with rapid cuts.
- Plan shot changes to land on the overlay word changes. The word and the cut should arrive together.

**Loop**
- Where possible, match the final frame's composition to the opening frame. A seamless loop encourages replays, which helps watch-through on short clips.

**Length and cost**
- Generation cost scales with clip length. A 15 second clip built from two generated shots of about 7 to 8 seconds each keeps each generation short and gives the cut point a natural place to land.

- Slow push ins, slow dollies, gentle tilts. No whip pans, orbits, or shake.

- Match final frame composition to opening frame where possible (loop).
