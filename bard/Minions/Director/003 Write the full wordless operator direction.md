**Director Brief: Korvus Operator, "The Watcher" (12 s, 9:16)**

**Concept:** One idea. While the trader sleeps, something keeps watch. A dark room, one glowing terminal, and an unblinking eye of light that never closes.

**Subject:** A single glowing terminal screen sitting alone in a dark bedroom at night. Its light is the only light in the frame. **Hook (0 to 1 s):** The frame opens on near total black. Within the first second, the pink glow ignites hard from the screen, a sharp pulse of light that spills across the room and wakes the frame. Motion is immediate, not a slow fade.

**Camera motion:**

- 1 to 6 s: slow push-in toward the screen, a steady dolly that narrows the frame onto the glow.
- 6 to 10 s: gentle lateral drift to the left, passing over the sleeping-space foreground (a blurred pillow edge, a dark sheet) so the scene reads as a bedroom at night, the trader absent from frame.

**Lighting:** Motivated only by the screen. Hot pink key from the terminal, soft pink bounce on the nearest wall, deep crushed black everywhere else. Subtle lens bloom on the brightest pixels. No warm practicals, no daylight, no window glow.

**Pacing:** Slow and deliberate after the hook. Light pulses roughly every 2 to 3 seconds, like a heartbeat or a scan line passing, never frantic. The final hold should feel like the watch continuing.

**Mood:** Quiet, vigilant, a little eerie in a calm way. Not hype, not urgency, no guru energy. The feeling is that something is awake for you. Honest and grounded, cinematic.

**Texture:** Subtle film grain, slight softness at the edges, shallow depth of field so the room falls away into black around the glow.

**Avoid:** No people on screen, no hands on keyboards, no faces, no money imagery, no implied returns or profit visuals, no competing light sources, no fast cuts.
