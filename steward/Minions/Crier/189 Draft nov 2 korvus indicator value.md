Prior day high and low are some of the most-watched levels on index futures, but a level is only useful if you trust how it was drawn. Before you rely on one, check these:

1. Session definition: confirm the day boundary matches your timezone and the session you actually trade.
2. Contract roll: make sure the levels come from the same contract you are charting, not an expired or back month.
3. Wick versus body: know whether your tool marks the true high and low or a closing-based reference.
4. Holiday and early close sessions: a shortened day can shift what "prior day" means.

Verify the levels against the bars yourself before you act on them.
