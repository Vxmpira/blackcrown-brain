**Four levels to mark before the open**

Before you look for anything, know where price has already been. These four levels are worth marking on every index futures chart:

1. **Prior day high:** the highest trade of the last session. When price returns to it, watch whether buyers defend that ground or let it go.
2. **Prior day low:** the mirror of the high. A clean break below it tells you the previous session's sellers are still in control.
3. **Session open:** the first print of the current session. It marks where the day started, so early trade above or below it reads differently.
4. **Prior week high:** the wider frame. Moves through it are slower to develop and tend to carry more weight than intraday noise.

Marking these is preparation, not a plan. Write down what you expect at each level before the bell, so you react to your notes instead of your nerves.

**Nov 4 ,  Discord value post**

**Level review: what happened the last time you were there**

Yesterday gave a lot of traders a clean test of a level. The useful part is not the touch itself. It is what happened after: did price hold, reject, or push straight through?

A simple way to review it:

- Note the level and the exact time price first touched it.
- Write down what the next 15 minutes did: absorption, a sharp rejection, or continuation.
- Compare the result to what you expected before the touch.

Most of the learning sits in the gap between expectation and result. Keep the notes short and honest, and review them weekly instead of in the heat of the session.
