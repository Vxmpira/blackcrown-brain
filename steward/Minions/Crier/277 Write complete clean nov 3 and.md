**Pre-session checklist: three levels to mark before the open**

Index futures tend to move between levels the whole room is watching. Before the bell, mark these three:

1. **Prior day high and low.** Yesterday's extremes are where stops and breakout orders often sit. Note whether price opens inside, above, or below them.

2. **Prior week high and low.** Weekly extremes carry more weight than daily ones. A session that trades near them deserves a closer look than one that stays in the middle of the range.

3. **Overnight session high and low.** The Globex range shows where overnight trading found acceptance or rejection. Price returning to the midpoint of that range reads differently than price breaking out of it.

Write the three prices on your chart before you look at the first candle of the open. Then watch what price does at each one: accepts, rejects, or sweeps and returns. Log what you see.

These are reference levels for study, not entries or targets. Nothing here is financial advice.

**Session review: ten minutes after the close**

Most traders review their losses. Fewer review their process. A short written review after each session builds more skill than any single trade.

Answer these five questions in a notepad or journal:

1. **What did price do at my marked levels?** List each level and whether it was accepted, rejected, or swept.

2. **Did I act on a plan or a reaction?** If you entered or exited, write the reason before you check the result.

3. **Where did I hesitate?** Hesitation usually points to a rule you have not written down yet.

4. **What would I change with the same information?** Be specific. "Be more patient" is not a change. "Wait for a close back inside the range before entering" is.

5. **What did I learn that I did not expect?** This is the question that improves the next session.

Keep the review under ten minutes and do it the same way every day. Patterns show up in two weeks of notes that never show up in a single memory of a bad afternoon.

This is education for your own study, not financial advice.
