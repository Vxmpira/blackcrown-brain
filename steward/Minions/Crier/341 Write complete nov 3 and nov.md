Nov 3: prior day high and low

Yesterday's high and low are two of the most watched reference points on an index futures chart. They come from the session itself, so everyone looking at the chart is working from the same lines.

What to watch: how price behaves on its first test. Does it trade through and hold, or trade through and snap back? Either outcome tells you something about who is in control at that level. Track what happens rather than assuming it.

LodeStone plots the prior day high and low from the prior day and week levels layer in its inputs panel. It does not repaint, so the lines stay where they printed.

Nov 6: prior session midpoint

Take the prior session's high and low and find the point halfway between them. That one number splits the range in two and gives you a clean reference for whether the last session was accepted above or below its middle.

What to watch: whether price spends time on one side of the midpoint or keeps crossing back through it. Repeated crossing reads differently than holding one side. Note what you see, then compare it to the open.

LodeStone does not plot the prior session midpoint yet. Draw it as a manual line from the prior session high and low, then check it alongside the prior day levels LodeStone does plot.

Not financial advice. Post your midpoint observations in #korvus-indicator.
