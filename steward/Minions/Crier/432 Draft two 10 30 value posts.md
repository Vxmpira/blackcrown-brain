**Post 1: Session levels primer**

Session levels, a quick primer for index futures.

Before the cash open, mark four levels on your chart: the prior day high and low, and the overnight high and low from the Asia and London sessions. Add the prior week high and low if your chart has room.

Price often reacts where liquidity sat before. A level tested across several sessions gets revisited more often than a random price. The useful question is not whether price reaches a level. It is what happens when it gets there: a clean break, a rejection, or a wick through and back.

Journal one line per day: which levels were touched and how price behaved at each. Two weeks of notes will teach you more than any single setup.

Which level do you check first?

**Post 2: Reviewing levels after the close**

Reviewing session levels after the close.

Pull up yesterday's chart and ask three questions. Which levels did price respect? Which did it run through without stopping? Did the reaction come at the session open or later?

You are looking for how a level behaves, not a rule to trade blindly. A level that gets swept and reclaimed tells a different story than one that gets accepted and held.
