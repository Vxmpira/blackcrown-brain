**Post 1 (Oct 30)**

**Know the clock before you read the chart**

Before you read price on an index future, check the USD economic agenda for your session. Many of the moves that catch traders off guard line up with a scheduled release, and the release time is public days in advance.

Two habits cost almost nothing. First, look at which high impact releases land inside your trading window. Second, convert the release time into your own timezone instead of trusting the one you assumed. A 8:30 Eastern release is a different event for a trader who has the clock wrong by an hour.

#forex-news posts the daily USD agenda, with live actuals as they print. Korvus, the market intelligence terminal, carries the same calendar context. Korvus is information, not trading advice, so treat the calendar as a starting point for your own decisions.

Which scheduled release do you watch most closely, and what do you do in the minutes before it hits?

**Post 2 (Oct 31), priority**

**Read the Presidential Wire fields in order**

Presidential Wire scores each news item on four things: impact, direction, confidence, and a written Korvus read. Most people skim the headline and jump to the direction arrow. A more useful habit is to read the fields in order, letting each one answer a different question.

Impact asks how much the item matters to index futures at all. Direction is the scored lean, not an instruction. Confidence shows how sure the scoring is, which is a reason to weigh the item, not a reason to act. The written read explains the reasoning, so you can agree or disagree with it on specifics.

Illustration: an item scored impact 4 of 5, direction down, confidence 62 percent. Read that as "worth a look, model moderately sure," then check the price context yourself.

Not financial advice. Scored items are information for your own process, and every decision stays with you.

Which field do you check first, and has a confidence score ever changed how much attention you gave a headline?
