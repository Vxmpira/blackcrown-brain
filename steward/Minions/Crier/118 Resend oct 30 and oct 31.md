**Oct 30, FRESH REPLACEMENT (not original)**

Session levels worth marking before the New York open

Most index futures traders already track the prior day high and low. Fewer track where the Asia and London ranges sit relative to them, and that gap is often where the morning's first move gets decided.

Try this on your own charts: plot the Asia high and low, the London high and low, and the prior day close. Then watch which levels price respects during the first hour of New York. Log what happened in your journal as a record of session behavior, not as a signal.

Over a few weeks you build a map of how these levels behave on your instrument. That map is worth more than any single call. LodeStone plots these ranges automatically if you want the chart done for you. Educational content only, not financial advice.

**Oct 31, FRESH REPLACEMENT (not original)**

Reading the USD agenda before you trade the open

Every morning #forex-news posts the USD economic agenda with live actuals as they print. The useful question is not only what the number was, but how far it landed from forecast and how price reacted in the next few minutes.

Try this on your next release: note the consensus, the actual, and the first five minute candle. Write down whether the move held or faded. After a month, you will see which releases your instrument trades cleanly and which ones chop, and that pattern matters more than any headline.
