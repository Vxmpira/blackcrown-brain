**Oct 30, #strategies**

Session levels are one of the simplest context tools on index futures, and they're worth marking before the open. Note the prior day's high and low, the overnight range, and the prior week's high and low on your chart. Then ask a plain question: when price returns to one of these levels, does it hold, break, or get swept and reclaimed? Logging a few sessions this way builds a reference for how the market has reacted at known levels, which is often more useful than any single setup. What has your own level log shown you, and which level type has been the most informative for you lately?

**Oct 31, #presidential-news**

Headlines move futures differently depending on whether the market already expected them. A useful habit is to separate three questions: how much the news could matter (impact), which way it leans for equities (direction), and how sure the read is (confidence). Illustration: a tariff headline that was widely priced in may produce a muted move, while the same headline as a surprise can gap the open. Scoring each headline on those three axes before reacting gives you a consistent process. What scoring habit do you use when a political headline hits during the session?
