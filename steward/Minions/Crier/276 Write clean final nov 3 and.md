**Three levels to mark before the open**

Most index futures traders I talk with run the same short checklist before the session starts. These are the three levels worth having on the chart first:

1. The prior day high and low. Where did yesterday's range end, and is price respecting or reclaiming either side?
2. The Asian session range. Did overnight build a box that the cash open is now testing?
3. The prior week high and low. Are we trading inside last week's range or pushing into new territory?

None of this tells you what to do. It gives the chart landmarks. Mark them first, then watch how price behaves when it reaches them.

**Reading a scored headline**

Presidential Wire posts each scored item with three fields: impact, direction, and confidence. They answer different questions, and it helps to keep them separate.

Impact is how much the event could matter to the market if it plays out as expected. Direction is which way the scoring model reads the likely move in the index futures. Confidence is how sure the model is about that read, based on how clearly the headline supports it.

A high impact item with low confidence usually means the event is important but the wording is vague. A high confidence item with low impact is often a clean read on something that may not move the tape much. Neither one is a signal on its own.

Korvus scores these items around the clock so you can compare them against your own read before the open, not after the move. The written Korvus read sits under each score so you can check the reasoning, not just the number. This is information and education, not financial advice.
