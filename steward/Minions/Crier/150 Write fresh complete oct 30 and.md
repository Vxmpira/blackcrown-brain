**Oct 30, #strategies**

**Session levels worth marking before the open**

Most index futures traders anchor their plan to a short list of levels the market already respects: the prior day high and low, the overnight high and low, the London range, and the prior week high and low. None of these need a special indicator. They need to be written down before the session starts, so you react to price at known places instead of inventing levels mid move.

A simple habit: mark each level, note whether price sits above or below it at the open, and log what happened the first time price tested it. Over a few weeks, that log shows you more about your own reactions than any single setup.

Education only, not a signal. Which levels matter is a decision for your own plan.

**Oct 31, #presidential-news**

**How to read a Presidential Wire item**

Each scored item gives you four things, and each answers a different question.

Impact: how much the headline could matter to index futures, from low to high. High impact does not mean a direction.

Direction: the lean the scoring assigns to the likely market reaction. It is a read to test, not a prediction to accept.

Confidence: how sure the scoring is about that lean. Low confidence is a reason to wait for price to confirm, not a reason to act.

Korvus read: the written summary that ties impact, direction, and confidence together in plain language.

Read the headline first, then check whether the three scores agree with what your chart is already showing. When they disagree, that gap is often the most useful thing to study.

Education only. Not financial advice.
