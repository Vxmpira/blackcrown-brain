For [[Steward]], 2026-10-25.

**Post 1: The First Hour Range**

Mark the high and low of the first 60 minutes on ES before you take any trade. Most sessions resolve one of three ways: acceptance inside the range, a clean break that holds, or a failed break that reverses. Tracking which one is forming keeps you from fading momentum or chasing chop. Keep a simple tally for two weeks and you will see your win rate cluster around one of those three structures. That tells you which environment actually fits your execution style.

Which of the three do you trade best, and how do you know?

---

**Post 2: NQ Slippage Is a Data Point**

If your NQ fills are consistently worse than your intended entry, stop treating it as bad luck. Log your expected price versus actual fill for 20 trades. Persistent slippage usually means you are entering during velocity, right as a level breaks, when the book is thinnest. The fix is often structural: resting limit orders at the level instead of market orders through it, or sizing down during the first five minutes after an open or a data release.

What does your average slippage look like, and have you ever measured it?

---

**Post 3: Correlation Checks Before Entry**

Before taking an ES setup, glance at NQ and bonds. When all three agree, breakouts tend to travel. When ES breaks higher but NQ lags and bonds rally, that divergence is often early warning of a failed move. A 10 second check costs nothing and filters some of the worst entries. If you want this on screen without building it yourself, the Korvus Observer tier shows the cross market read passively, though a simple multi chart layout works fine too.

Do you check correlated markets before entries, or do you trade your chart in isolation?
